Valuing the Run

How the model works

Every football metric credits the player who touches the ball. The player who made the run — who timed the movement in behind, who pulled a defender across — gets nothing. This rebuilds the run itself from camera data, and puts a value on it. Built from 93,202 reconstructed runs across 127 matches.

2
The trap we avoid — why the obvious version is not a model
Don't Look up threat at the end, subtract threat at the start

That is two lookups on a grid, subtracted. It is arithmetic, not modelling — nothing is fitted, nothing is predicted, and it cannot see a single defender. Two runs ending on the same blade of grass score identically, whether the runner was alone or surrounded by three.

Expected threat (xT)

Kept in the output as a plain-English companion — it explains in one sentence — but clearly labelled as descriptive, not as the model.

3
What we actually model — one fitted function, used twice
V(state) = chance the team shoots within the next 5 seconds

Given where the ball is, where the player is, and the defenders around both. Because a run moves the team from one state to another, the run's value is simply the change: ΔV = V(after) − V(before).

Gradient boosting (LightGBM)Out-of-fold GroupKFold by match

Seconds, not "next 5 actions". Five short passes take three seconds; five duels take thirty. For judging a run, real time is the honest clock.

Read this twice The headline number is not a probability

Two scouts watch the same move. A sees two dots — where it started, where the ball arrived. B sees those dots plus the run: distance, speed, whether he shook his marker, how many defenders he beat. Both answer "is this attack going somewhere?" — and the metric is how much B's answer differs from A's.

So it measures how much the manner of the movement explains, over and above the geography of it. Not P(goal), which is xG. Not P(shot), which is V. Not a value on a patch of grass, which is xT. It is a difference between two estimates — which is why it can go negative, and why it cannot be added to goals.

Separating the run from the pass — the correction that mattered most
Problem ΔV credits the runner for the passer's work

Busquets — who does not make off-ball runs — appeared 10th. ΔV moves with how far the ball travelled (r = 0.14) almost exactly as much as with how far the player did (r = 0.15). It is a joint measure.

Fix Fit the model twice, keep only the difference

Once without the run features, once with them, on identical rows and folds. What the run features add is the run's own contribution.

And what that costs The ordering is target-sensitive

Because the metric is the share of the outcome explained by movement rather than position, the split between the two depends on what you ask the model to explain. Four targets were tested on identical rows and folds. Only one produces an ordering with face validity — and it was chosen on that basis, which is weak evidence.

So: shortlist profiles with it, do not separate 11th from 14th. The fix, not yet built, is a counterfactual — hold the ball and defenders fixed and move only the player, so it cannot proxy for phase.

What it assumes

That the nearest team-mate to the reception point is the player who ran. The end of the run is measured exactly; the start is inferred and gated.

What it cannot see

Runs that never received the ball. The decoy that drags a centre-back away and is never picked out is invisible, so selfless runners are under-credited.

Where to be careful

Four club seasons, four leagues. The method is defensible; the exact order is target-dependent. Use it to shortlist profiles, not to separate adjacent ranks — and compare within a squad more confidently than across leagues.
